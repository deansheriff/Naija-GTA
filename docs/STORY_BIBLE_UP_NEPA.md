## Premise and player promise

A lavish wedding loses power. When the lights flicker back, a fictional politician's campaign bag has vanished. A danfo driver finds the bag, a canteen owner's employee is connected to its journey, and her content-creator nephew has recorded evidence that powerful people would prefer to erase. None of the three planned a robbery. Each has something to hide, someone to protect, and a different idea of what “doing the right thing” costs.

Play a warm, loud comedy adventure about three people improvising their way through a city that never waits for them. Switch perspectives to solve problems through driving and passenger arguments, favors and negotiation, or filming and crowd reactions. The comedy comes from competing priorities; underneath it is a story about trust, public accountability and the difference between getting ahead and leaving people behind.

The opening question is “Who has the bag?” The lasting question is “Who will stand beside you when the light returns?”

## Themes and tone

- **Visibility versus reputation:** KC can get everyone watching without helping them understand. A public story is not necessarily the truth.
- **Debt versus care:** Ngozi remembers what people owe because her workers and savings-circle members depend on her. Forgiveness has a cost; so does never offering it.
- **Shortcuts versus responsibility:** Tunde wants a fleet, but his passengers need this one bus to arrive safely today.
- **Community versus private escape:** Shared infrastructure, private wealth and leaving the city offer different kinds of freedom. No ending erases consequences.

Comedy first, heart underneath. Power cuts, delayed payments, family requests, dress-code panic and transport arguments interrupt grand plans. Punch at power, bureaucracy and self-deception. Never make ethnicity, religion, disability or poverty the punchline. Action is cartoonish, recoverable and without gore. Serious moments get room to breathe instead of a joke after every line.

## Tunde “Baba God Dey” Adeyemi · 34

**Public face:** a loud danfo philosopher who can explain any bad route as an excellent idea. His battered yellow bus is named *Baba God Dey*. His dream is *Baba God Dey Express*, a fleet that finally makes people take him seriously.

**Voice:** short declarations, elaborate traffic metaphors, affectionate insults between familiar adults, and arguments that become speeches. He talks to the bus as if it is a difficult younger brother. His confidence rises exactly when his plan gets worse.

**Want / need:** wants independence and a fleet; needs to accept that leadership means caring for the people on board, not merely arriving first.

**Flaw and secret:** cannot resist a shortcut. He has been postponing a necessary repair and hiding how precarious the bus business is. The bag looks like the solution he has been praying for, which makes him vulnerable to rationalizing its use.

**Arc:** accidental custodian → self-appointed mastermind → dependable protector. His decisive growth is choosing a slower, safer route when someone else bears the risk of his shortcut.

**Play identity:** driving, timing, passenger persuasion. **Blood Pressure** is a clearly fictional stress meter, not a medical simulation. **Go-Slow Whisperer** briefly reveals authored bypasses; **Conductor Combo** turns a passenger argument into cooperation. Route and ability effects require explicit rules, not random narrative overrides.

**Recurring gag:** passengers debate every turn as if they have shares in the steering wheel. A confident speech ends with the bus refusing to start.

**Catchphrase drafts:** “This road is only a suggestion.” / “Baba God Dey; mechanic also dey.” Any localized delivery needs **LANG-REVIEW**.

## Ngozi “Aunty Boss” Eze · 41

**Public face:** canteen owner, POS agent and savings-circle organizer. She knows everyone's order, family circumstances and unpaid balance. She dreams of a catering business and hotel bearing her name.

**Voice:** precise, unhurried and devastatingly polite. She negotiates with powerful people as if they are bargaining over tomatoes. Her threats are mostly practical: no credit, no lunch, no convenient explanation.

**Want / need:** wants financial security and an empire; needs to distinguish protecting a community from controlling every member of it.

**Flaw and secret:** cannot leave a debt unresolved, even during a crisis. She has quietly covered a shortfall to protect a savings-circle member, while publicly insisting that rules apply equally. The Chief owes her business money for catering; his apparent respect has always been transactional.

**Arc:** collector and reluctant guardian → network leader → someone who can choose mercy without surrendering accountability. Her strongest ending gives her people agency instead of simply making them owe her.

**Play identity:** negotiation, investigation and community contacts. **Aunty Aura** measures confidence and earned standing. **The Stare** opens authored confession/negotiation choices; it does not magically force every character to obey. **Ajo Network** unlocks trusted transport, food, safe locations and information through established favors.

**Recurring gag:** she interrupts dramatic villain speeches to correct an invoice. After a devastating argument, she asks whether everyone has eaten.

**Catchphrase drafts:** “Eat first. Explain yourself after.” / “Your title is long. Your payment is late.” Local language and cultural delivery need **LANG-REVIEW**.

## Kelechi “KC Vibes” Nwosu · 23

**Public face:** Ngozi's nephew, a charming skit maker presenting an Island lifestyle from a crowded mainland apartment. He owes Tunde bus fares, wants to go viral and hopes to leave the country.

**Voice:** a polished presenter voice that collapses into anxious honesty. He turns errands into episode titles and mistakes silence for an audience waiting for a punchline.

**Want / need:** wants fame and an exit; needs to decide what he owes the people who make his life possible.

**Flaw and secret:** cannot resist the perfect shot. He edited out an embarrassing portion of the wedding footage before realizing it also hid a useful clue. Admitting the edit might destroy the credibility he has just gained.

**Arc:** narrator of other people's lives → frightened witness → participant who takes responsibility for his own edit, even when the truth costs him attention or an opportunity.

**Play identity:** filming, crowd reading, timing and evidence. **Hype** can open doors and attract unwanted attention. **Phone Battery** creates playable pressure during missions; it never drains while the game is closed. **Go Live** changes authored crowd responses; **Gist** changes fictional rumor flags with identifiable sources and consequences. Recording a conversation is not proof that every statement is true.

**Recurring gag:** his signal fails exactly when his introduction becomes most impressive. A plea to subscribe meets an entirely practical request for a charging cable.

**Catchphrase drafts:** “Content is loading. Courage is buffering.” / “Aunty, let me explain without the comments.” Localized versions need **LANG-REVIEW**.

## Relationships, antagonist and secrets

Tunde and Ngozi trade practical help and very precise complaints. He owes her trust before he owes her money. Ngozi is KC's aunt and safety net; KC mistakes her protection for disbelief in his ambitions. Tunde sees KC as an unreliable passenger until the young man chooses to put down the phone and help. KC sees Tunde as a comic character until the driver's risk becomes real to him.

**Chief Dr. Sir Barr. Olusegun “Double Chop” Adebayo-Williams** is an invented candidate for the fictional Agbado-Ketu Central council. His endless titles and carefully staged generosity hide a ledger of promises made to incompatible supporters. His comedy comes from an obsession with appearances and a staff unable to fit his full title on anything. He is not a portrait of a real politician.

The missing bag contains cash-like fictional story props and, more significantly, a diary of receipts and promises. The old notes are a narrative reveal, not a claim about real currency. The diary rewards investigation: fragments are discoverable before the ending, and the player can choose to preserve evidence instead of treating the bag only as spending power.

The unnamed canteen employee in the seed becomes **Nneka**, a fictional adult catering worker whose account is initially dismissed because the three leads argue over her. She gets an intelligible motive and testimony; she is neither a disposable suspect nor a surprise villain solely for convenience. Her exact role in the bag's transfer is settled in the mission outline after review.

Existing sandbox neighbors Amaka and Yemi retain their identities and starter activities. Ngozi does not silently replace Amaka. Their later campaign appearances need deliberate roles, rather than assuming every canteen is the same business.

## Dramatic spine and endings

**Prologue · Owambe, Owambe:** introduce the three perspectives at a wedding through low-stakes social and timing problems. The blackout links their errands. The light returns, the bag is missing, and the title lands. This is a playable tutorial target, not a video-only requirement.

**Act 1 · Light Out:** discover the bag, establish Ngozi's obligations, protect KC's footage and bring the leads together at the canteen. Their first “Family Meeting” creates a pact that none of them interprets quite the same way.

**Act 2 · Spread:** the pact is tested by transport trouble, family events, fuel and generator pressure, reputation and conflicting ambitions. Every lead must create a problem another lead is better placed to solve. Replace the seed's cash-splitting/evasion premise with a fictional **receipt-and-witness collection** problem: collect evidence of unpaid obligations and return disputed property through accountable choices. No real-world laundering procedure is modeled or taught.

**Act 3 · Election Day:** a wholly fictional council contest culminates in an authored public exposure timed with power restoration. Evidence, alliances and earlier choices determine who believes the reveal. This campaign simulation is separate from the current private practice poll and from actual elections or real votes.

| Ending | Payoff and cost |
|---|---|
| Honest | Accountable recovery supports a community transformer and borehole. The leads remain financially stretched but trusted; their practical ambitions continue. |
| Hustle | Keeping the bag buys apparent freedom and creates obligations, conflict and damaged trust. Wealth is not a clean escape from consequences. |
| Japa | KC leaves after an earned opportunity. The farewell is funny and painful; Tunde and Ngozi continue, and KC's choice does not erase their agency. |
| Receipts, the secret ending | Preserved evidence reveals the diary's value and the old-note deception. Requires visible, discoverable investigation choices, not an arbitrary hidden button. |

## World and switching rules

The fictional city follows everyday emotional logic, not a documentary claim about Nigerian institutions. Invent names for in-world utilities, wardens, banks and telecoms. “Up NEPA!” remains the requested title/exclamation; no real utility logo or institutional endorsement is implied. No real politicians, fabricated quotes from real people, licensed music or live news are bundled by this plan.

**Family Meeting** is the switching interface. Short authored exchanges establish where each lead is and why a switch is possible. Missions can lock switching with a clear reason. Unplayed leads may get bounded, authored catch-up incidents—an argument, an embarrassing clip, a tow notice—but never silently spend money, lose critical evidence or invalidate a mission. Incidents must be reproducible, summarized and recoverable.

Campaign state lives in a separate, versioned save section with checkpoints, protagonist selection, evidence, relationship flags and ending choices. Existing sandbox balances, starter-gig rewards and characters remain separate. Consequential online actions use server validation and idempotent receipts. Cosmetic chat remains local. Mission-critical logic is scripted and works without Jev or news services.

Cutscenes use in-engine staging with text and a skip/summary path. Low-bandwidth mode keeps essential dialogue and objective cues without downloading cinematic videos. Subtitles, readable text and reduced effects are baseline requirements; voice acting is optional. Native-speaker review must cover every **LANG-REVIEW** line and cultural gag before release.
