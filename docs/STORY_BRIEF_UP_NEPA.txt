PROJECT: NAIJA STREET LIFE — STORY MODE
TITLE OF THE STORY: "UP NEPA!"
FULL BILLING: "Naija Street Life: Up NEPA!"
TAGLINE: "When the light comes back, everybody will see what you did."

NAMING
The platform is called "Naija Street Life." Use that name in all documents,
file names, UI text and code. The reference game lagoslife.app is a
competitor we studied; never use its name or branding for ours.

ROLE
You are the lead narrative designer and gameplay writer for Naija Street Life,
a Nigerian open-world comedy-crime game. Think GTA V's three-protagonist
structure, but the energy is pure Lagos: loud, quick-witted, chaotic, warm, and
funny at every turn. Your job is to turn the seed below into a full story mode
that players will quote on WhatsApp.

TONE
- Comedy first, with heart underneath. The player should laugh every few
  minutes and care about these people by the end.
- Humor comes from character, situation and Nigerian everyday absurdity
  (power cuts, go-slow, POS network failure, fuel queues, aso-ebi pressure,
  prosperity-gospel radio, relatives with "small request").
- Punch up and sideways: at big men, bureaucracy, hustle culture and
  self-deception. Do not mock ethnic groups, religions, disabilities or poverty.
- Dialogue mixes English, Pidgin, and light Yoruba/Igbo/Hausa where natural.
  Keep it readable for non-native players. Mark all slang for a native-speaker
  review before shipping.
- Action stays cartoonish and slapstick: vehicular chaos, chases, arguments
  that turn into brawls, never gore.

THE SETUP
A lavish Lekki owambe wedding. Mid-ceremony, NEPA takes the light. In the
two seconds of blackout, a Ghana-must-go bag holding the campaign war chest of
Chief Dr. Sir Barr. Olusegun "Double Chop" Adebayo-Williams (a fictional,
many-titled politician running for Local Government Chairman of the fictional
Agbado-Ketu Central) vanishes. The light returns, the whole hall shouts
"UP NEPA!", and the Chief's men start searching for the bag.

Our three heroes each touch the mess by accident:
- The bag ends up under a seat in a danfo.
- The danfo's passenger was a staff member of a canteen owner who is owed
  money by the Chief.
- A broke content creator livestreamed the whole blackout and his video shows
  faces. The Chief's people now want the bag, the phone and the people.

THE THREE PROTAGONISTS (switchable, like GTA V)

1) TUNDE "BABA GOD DEY" ADEYEMI, 34, danfo driver
- Owns one rattling yellow bus named "Baba God Dey," held together by prayer,
  tape and spite. Dream: a fleet called "Baba God Dey Express."
- Personality: loud, fearless, philosophical about traffic, argues about
  football and politics with every passenger. Allergic to road wardens.
- Flaw: cannot resist a shortcut, even when it is on fire.
- Stat: Blood Pressure. It rises with go-slow, wardens and bad passengers.
- Special ability: "Go-Slow Whisperer." Time slows and hidden bypass routes
  light up; passengers can be talked into anything via the Conductor Combo.
- Signature gag: passengers continue loud arguments in the back during
  action scenes and sometimes help or sabotage the player.
- Music: Fuji and street Afrobeats.

2) NGOZI "AUNTY BOSS" EZE, 41, canteen queen, POS agent, ajo organizer
- Runs a mama-put canteen, a POS point and a rotating savings circle that
  intimidates banks. Dream: a catering empire and a hotel with her name on it.
- Personality: motherly, ruthless, can end a man with one sentence and then
  feed him. Everyone owes her money; she remembers the exact amount.
- Flaw: cannot forgive a debt, even a ₦200 one, even during a heist.
- Stat: Aunty Aura. High aura makes NPCs back down, haggle or confess.
- Special ability: "The Stare" (NPCs freeze, confess or hand over the thing)
  and "Ajo Network" (call in favors from market women across the city: fast
  travel by "a trusted keke," hide-outs, free food, intel).
- Signature gag: she negotiates with villains like she is selling tomatoes.
- Music: Highlife and gospel-tinged Afrobeats.

3) KELECHI "KC VIBES" NWOSU, 23, aspiring skit maker and influencer
- Lives in a crowded Yaba apartment, pretends to live on the Island, is
  Aunty Boss's nephew and owes Tunde three months of bus fares.
- Dream: go viral and japa. Every plan is a content plan.
- Personality: charming, anxious, always filming, genuinely brave at the worst
  possible moment.
- Flaw: cannot resist a perfect shot, even when running for his life.
- Stat: Hype and Phone Battery. Battery drains constantly. NEPA never helps.
- Special ability: "Go Live" (filming makes crowds react, rally or turn on
  an enemy; high Hype opens doors) and "Gist" (spreads rumors that change what
  NPCs believe about a person or event).
- Signature gag: narrates his life like a skit, begs strangers to "like and
  subscribe," and keeps losing signal at dramatic moments.
- Music: Amapiano and street-pop.

SWITCHING MECHANIC
Players switch characters through a group chat called "Family Meeting 🔥."
Switching plays a short funny chat exchange ("Where are you?" "Dey come."
"Which one is 'dey come'?"). Unplayed characters keep living. When the player
switches back, something funny or terrible has happened (the danfo got
towed, Aunty Boss started a dispute, KC went viral for the wrong reason).

PLOT STRUCTURE

PROLOGUE: "Owambe, Owambe" (tutorial, play all three)
Money-spraying mechanic, aso-ebi dress-code fashion check, jollof queue
politics, MC chaos, then the blackout. The crowd shouts "UP NEPA!" as the
light snaps on for two seconds, the bag is gone, and the title card drops:
"Naija Street Life: Up NEPA!"

ACT 1: "Light Out" (each hero gets an intro, then they collide)
- Tunde: "Baba God Dey." A day of passengers with strong opinions. He finds
  the bag when a passenger skips paying "because I no get change."
- Aunty Boss: "Ajo Day." Collect contributions from stubborn debtors; the POS
  network keeps failing ("Network don fail, come back tomorrow").
- KC: "Four Percent." His livestream has 40,000 viewers, his phone is at 4%
  and there is no light to charge it. A frantic hunt for power.
- Convergence: "Family Meeting." All three argue in Aunty Boss's canteen over
  generator noise about what to do with the bag and who is guilty.

ACT 2: "Spread" (solo arcs, co-op missions, escalating chaos)
Example missions:
- "Generator Wars": a night run across the city to move a generator and
  diesel while rival neighborhoods defend theirs.
- "Japa Interview": KC faces a visa-interview mini-game with an absurd
  officer; the stakes decide whether he leaves or stays.
- "Danfo Chase": Tunde is chased through go-slow with a bus full of
  opinionated passengers who each vote on every turn.
- "Aso-Ebi Infiltration": the heroes sneak into the Chief's mother's 90th
  birthday party dressed as guests, with strict dress codes and relatives who
  ask personal questions.
- "Small Small Money": the bag's cash must be moved through hundreds of POS
  agents in small amounts so the bank does not flag it, using Aunty Boss's
  network.
- "Fuel Queue Standoff": a negotiation and slapstick brawl at a petrol
  station.
- "Viral": KC's Gist and Go Live change public opinion, and the news-driven
  gossip system reacts.

ACT 3: "Election Day"
The Chief's election is live (hook into the poll system). Rumors, real
votes, bribes and promises swing the result. Finale: "Up NEPA!" The heroes
time a power restoration at the exact moment to expose the Chief live on
stage, with every light, speaker and phone on.

ENDINGS (player's choices decide)
A) Honest: the money is returned to the community as a transformer and
   borehole; the heroes stay broke but respected.
B) Hustle: the player keeps the bag; money and trouble multiply, friendships
   crack.
C) Japa: KC leaves; Tunde and Aunty Boss's story continues; sad-funny.
D) Secret ending: the bag's real prize is the Chief's diary of receipts and
   promises, and the money was mostly old notes.

SAMPLE SCENE (use for tone)
INT. OWAMBE HALL — NIGHT
MC: (on mic) Ladies and gentlemen, the groom's mother will now dance for the
third time!
[POWER CUTS. Darkness. A beat.]
CROWD: NEPAAAAA!
MC: Please remain calm, we have a generator!
[Long pause. Distant chugging.]
MC: ...The generator is also in the dark.
[LIGHT SNAPS ON for two seconds. The bag is gone. DARK again.]
TUNDE: (shouting) Madam, I carry people, I no carry bag!
AUNTY BOSS: (calm, from the dark) Whoever is holding my ₦8,000, I can see you.
KC: (whispering, filming) Guys, this is not a drill. Smash like and subscribe.
[LIGHT RETURNS. Everyone is frozen mid-pose.]
CROWD: UP NEPAAAA!

SYSTEM INTEGRATION (tie into the rest of Naija Street Life)
- Job/gig board: each hero has side hustles (Tunde: ride/delivery gigs;
  Aunty Boss: catering and POS gigs; KC: content and skit gigs).
- Poll/election system: powers Act 3 and optional side elections.
- Jev AI: handles ambient NPC decisions and side-mission improvisation.
  Story-critical NPCs and beats stay scripted so the plot cannot break.
- News API: radio, WhatsApp gist and NPC banter react to real events; story
  scenes must never depend on them.
- Data/airtime mini-games: reuse story mini-games (visa interview, ajo
  collection, danfo conversation) as reward games.
- Graphics settings: provide a low-bandwidth cutscene mode.
- Assets (Blender): the danfo "Baba God Dey," the Ghana-must-go bag, Aunty
  Boss's POS umbrella, KC's ring light and tripod, generators, owambe hall,
  and the three hero models.

CONTENT RULES
- All people, brands, agencies and places are fictional. Use invented names
  for banks, telecoms, road wardens and government bodies.
- No real politicians, no invented quotes from real people.
- No glorifying fraud or scams. Hustlers can be scrappy but the story should
  not teach crime.
- Keep violence slapstick and out of gore.
